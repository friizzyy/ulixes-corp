What the work produces

Eight programs, six instrument classes.

3 PROGRAMSEarnings and auditWhere accounting treatment decides what the numbers say.

Designation, effectiveness testing, and the documentation that has to hold when a reviewer pulls the file. The framework decides which period a gain lands in, so a design that is merely defensible on paper still moves earnings if the system implements it differently.

Finding where P&L timing is being set by configuration rather than by intent. Volatility that nobody chose is almost always a designation, a curve, or a booking model doing something the desk never asked it to do.

The evidence trail a reviewer asks for, assembled before they ask for it. Written so the CFO can explain the architecture to analysts and the CAO can defend the treatment without reopening the implementation.

3 PROGRAMSDelivery and migrationWhere the platform changes hands or changes shape.

Product coverage, configuration, and delivery sequenced across all three offices at once. The sequencing is the work: a front-office decision taken in month one sets the accounting treatment nobody looks at until month nine.

Position, trade, and static data mapped to the target model and reconciled against it rather than assumed. Migrations rarely fail on cutover weekend. They fail at the first month end.

Coverage shaped by lifecycle event rather than by screen. Happy-path testing says nothing about maturity, early termination, counterparty default, or a market that moves.

2 PROGRAMSCapital and regulatoryWhere a booking decision becomes a reported one.

How a trade is booked, netted, and classified, and what each of those choices consumes. The same economic position can carry materially different capital depending on decisions taken at configuration time.

Submissions that can be traced back to the transaction that produced them, field by field. The hard half is being able to answer, months later, why a specific number was what it was.
